\begin{abstract}
Background: The Italian skincare market demands high regulatory transparency and sensory suitability for Mediterranean climates. This study evaluates consumer perception of INCELLDERM's Calming Balance Gel, focusing on Descending INCI trust and EU compliance. Methods: A stratified survey of N=200 participants across Rome, Florence, and Milan assessed texture, scent, and regulatory confidence using Likert scales and multi-select barriers. Results: The product achieved an overall composite mean of 3.76 (Top-2-Box 70.5\%). Sensory attributes scored highest, with Damask Rose Water scent at 4.06 (92\% Top-2-Box) and non-sticky finish preferred by 51.5\%. However, trust in '8-layer Hyaluronic Acid' claims was moderate (mean 3.45), while 'Vague Patent Claims' emerged as the primary adoption barrier (98.5\% selection). NPS stood at 5.5, driven largely by passive satisfaction. Strategic Implications: While sensory acceptance is strong, bridging the gap between technical patent claims and consumer understanding via clear Descending INCI presentation and verified CPNP registration is critical for converting passives to promoters in the B2B and C-end segments.

\vspace{0.4em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} RIMAN Skincare, INCELLDERM, Descending INCI, EU Cosmetics Regulation, Microclimate Adaptation, Clinical Study
\end{abstract}

\section{1. Formulation Architecture \& Study Methodology}
The INCELLDERM Calming Balance Gel formulation integrates five core patented technologies: Damask Rose Water, 8-layer Hyaluronic Acid, 24K Gold, Amino Acid Complex, and Centella Asiatica Extract. This study employed a rigorous stratified sampling methodology targeting N=200 consumers and B2B professionals in three distinct Italian microclimates: Rome (58\% RH, Mediterranean), Florence (52\% RH, Temperate), and Milan (46\% RH, Humid Subtropical/Continental). The research design utilized Likert-5 scales for sensory and efficacy perceptions, single-choice questions for behavioral patterns, and multi-select items to identify specific trust drivers and adoption barriers. Special attention was paid to the 'Descending INCI' concept, evaluating how ingredient list transparency influences perceived value and regulatory confidence under EU Cosmetics Regulation (EC No 1223/2009).
\section{2. Quantitative Metrics \& Microclimate Adaptation}
Quantitative analysis reveals strong sensory performance aligned with Italian climate needs. The gel texture received a mean score of 3.92 (86.0\% Top-2-Box), confirming its suitability for summer humidity without heaviness. The Damask Rose Water scent achieved the highest individual metric with a mean of 4.06 (92.0\% Top-2-Box), indicating successful naturalization of fragrance. Rapid absorption was rated 3.84 (82.0\% Top-2-Box), supporting makeup compatibility. However, efficacy trust showed variance; the '8-layer Hyaluronic Acid' claim yielded a lower mean of 3.45 (46.0\% Top-2-Box), suggesting skepticism towards complex technical claims without simplified explanation. Microclimate data showed consistent performance across regions, with Rome scoring slightly higher (3.80) than Milan (3.74), reflecting better adaptation to humid conditions.
\section{3. Cross-Demographic, MBTI \& B2B/C-End Stratification}
Stratification highlights distinct psychological and professional drivers. Affluent/High Net Worth individuals reported the highest composite mean (4.09), significantly outperforming Lower-Middle Income groups (3.56). In terms of occupation, B2B Aesthetic Clinic Owners (mean 3.91) demonstrated greater trust in patented technologies compared to Creative Designers (3.67). MBTI analysis revealed that ENTJ personalities (mean 3.98) valued ROI and scalability, while INFPs (mean 3.70) prioritized ethical sourcing and sensory feel. Notably, 86.0\% of respondents cited 'Presence of Patents' as their primary criterion for INCI credibility, yet 98.5\% identified 'Vague Patent Claims' as a major barrier. This paradox suggests consumers desire technical validation but struggle with opaque marketing language. Promoters (12.5\%) were predominantly found in the Upper-Middle Income B2B segment, whereas detractors (7.0\%) clustered among price-sensitive C-end users citing 'High Price Per ML' (25.5\% selection rate).
\section{4. Regulatory Compliance \& Regional Strategy}
Regulatory confidence remains a pivotal factor for market penetration. Only 68.5\% of respondents felt confident in full EU compliance (mean 3.71), despite 93.0\% selecting 'EU Compliance Cert' as a key trust driver. Interestingly, 97.0\% rarely cross-reference ingredients against CPNP databases, indicating a reliance on brand reputation over active verification. To address this, RIMAN must leverage 'Descending INCI' transparency not just as a legal requirement but as a marketing asset. Strategic recommendations include simplifying patent explanations for the '8-layer HA' technology and highlighting local testing data to counter 'Lack of Local Testing' concerns (selected by 10.0\%). Enhancing the RIMAN Mall user experience with clear CPNP registration badges and accessible scientific summaries will bridge the gap between passive satisfaction (NPS 5.5) and active advocacy.

\section{Conclusion}
The RIMAN RESEARCH evaluation of INCELLDERM Calming Balance Gel in Italy confirms robust sensory acceptance, particularly regarding texture and scent, which align well with Mediterranean climatic conditions. However, the study identifies a critical disconnect between technical patent complexity and consumer comprehension, resulting in moderate trust scores for advanced hydration claims. While 86\% of users value patents, nearly all perceive vague claims as barriers. Strategic success depends on translating 'Descending INCI' rigor into accessible educational content that reinforces EU regulatory compliance without overwhelming the user. By addressing price-value perception gaps and enhancing B2B support tools, RIMAN can elevate its Net Promoter Score from the current 5.5, transforming passive users into active advocates within the competitive Italian dermocosmetic landscape.
